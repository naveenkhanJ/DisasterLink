# Use Case: UC4 Analyse Disaster Response & Generate Reports
**Use Case Scenario:** After the Kalu Ganga flood of 20–25 May 2026, a DMC Officer reviews the flood response in Ratnapura and Kalutara, records the findings, exports the report as a PDF and shares a scoped report with CARE Sri Lanka

*Primary actor: DMC Officer. Secondary actor: Resource-Owning Organisation (government bodies, armed forces, NGOs, private donors, community volunteer groups) — receives the shared, scoped report; on the use case diagram it is associated with Export or Share Report (steps 12–19), not with UC4 directly. Included use cases: Select Event, District and Period; Calculate Response Statistics; Generate Statistical Report (steps 8–12). Extension point: `export or share requested` (declared on the included Generate Statistical Report, at step 12; extended by Export or Share Report). UC4 itself declares no extension point.*

**Description:**
This use case scenario describes how a DMC Officer at the National Emergency Operations Centre reviews a disaster response after an event: the officer selects the report criteria (districts, hazard type, period and metrics, optionally pre-filled from a closed hazard event or a saved criteria template), and the system checks them against the officer's reporting districts, computes the alert timeline, the citizens reached, shelter occupancy over time and relief distribution from the stored records, and stores the report with its results, against which the officer records findings. The officer then exports the report for DMC officials (PDF, Excel or CSV) and shares it with Resource-Owning Organisations, each of which sees it with the distribution table restricted to its own lines; UC4 is used online from the DMC and has no offline flow (CH-05).

*Conventions:* The criteria panel and its pick-lists are parts of the screen; the panel keeps the values already entered when it is shown again.

**Preconditions:**
- The DMC Officer's reporting districts are recorded (here Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara, Ratnapura and Kegalle; an officer with no recorded districts reports nationally).
- Hazards, warnings and their escalations, notification deliveries, shelter occupancy records and relief distributions recorded through UC1, Escalate Warning, UC3 and the district officers are stored on the server. Here the hazard "Kalu Ganga flood" (HZ-2026-0142, Flood, Ratnapura and Kalutara, from 20 May 2026 09:30) is closed (25 May 2026); its warning WRN-2026-0087 was issued at Level 2 – Amber on 20 May 2026 at 10:45 (UC1) and escalated to Level 3 – Red on 21 May 2026 at 08:25 (Escalate Warning). Some clients may still hold unsynchronised items (A3).
- Every relief supply has one recorded owner: a Resource-Owning Organisation, or a citizen (shown as "Private individual").
- The officer's workstation is online.

**Main Flow:**
1. DMC Officer opens the analytics workspace.
2. System shows the report criteria panel with the officer's saved criteria templates and the closed hazard events, among them "HZ-2026-0142 · Flood — Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura · Ratnapura, Kalutara · 20–25 May 2026 · CLOSED" (the Kalu Ganga flood hazard; screens label a hazard by its type and location name — MD C2, FX-14).
3. DMC Officer selects the closed event HZ-2026-0142 (the Kalu Ganga flood hazard) to pre-fill the criteria (*Select Event, District and Period*).
4. System pre-fills the criteria from the event — districts Ratnapura and Kalutara (the hazard's districts), hazard type Flood, period 20–25 May 2026 (the hazard's start and closing dates) and a reference to the event — and shows them in the criteria panel.
5. DMC Officer selects districts, hazard type and period, optionally pre-filled from a closed event: here the officer keeps the pre-filled values, selects the metrics (alert timeline, citizens reached, shelter occupancy over time, resource distribution) and submits the criteria.
6. System validates the criteria: at least one district, a start date not after the end date, a period of at most 24 months, and at least one metric. It also checks every selected district against the officer's reporting districts. Ratnapura and Kalutara are both within the officer's scope, so the criteria are valid.
7. System computes each selected metric with that metric's own calculator (*Calculate Response Statistics*). Every calculator applies the whole criteria (districts, hazard type and period) to its retrieval, and only the selected metrics are computed:
   - **Alert timeline** — the warnings of Flood hazards that cover at least one selected district and were issued in the period, with their escalations, in time order: 20 May 2026 10:45 · Issued · WRN-2026-0087 · Level 2 – Amber (Be Alert) · Ratnapura, Kalutara; 21 May 2026 08:25 · Escalated · WRN-2026-0087 · Level 3 – Red (Evacuate) · Ratnapura, Kalutara. The escalation entry is taken from the warning's "warning escalated" audit record, which holds the escalation time and the old and new levels (Level 2 – Amber → Level 3 – Red). The warning itself now holds Level 3 – Red, so the issue entry takes its level from the old level of the warning's earliest escalation record (a warning never escalated shows its current level).
   - **Citizens reached** — the distinct citizens registered in a selected district with at least one delivered notification of the matching warnings (those of the alert timeline; here WRN-2026-0087's Level 2 issue and its Level 3 escalation), per channel (Push 30,100; SMS 42,335; Audible alert 30,100) and in total (42,335). The total counts distinct citizens, so a citizen reached on several channels, or by both the issue and the escalation, counts once; it is not the sum of the channels. Organisations and district officers are not counted as citizens.
   - **Shelter occupancy over time** — the occupancy records captured in the period at the matching shelters; a shelter matches when it is in a selected district and has at least one occupancy record captured in the period. Each record is placed by its capture time (e.g. an update captured offline in Kalutara at 14:05 on 21 May and received at 14:40 is placed at 14:05). The occupancy of the selected districts at any time is the sum of each matching shelter's latest recorded occupancy at or before that time, plotted against the combined capacity — the current capacity of the matching shelters (19 shelters, 6,300 places, including Kalutara Town Hall registered during the event; peak 5,920 occupants on 21 May, 94 %, after the Level 3 – Red escalation). The hazard type filters a shelter through the warnings it was allocated to and their hazards. A shelter allocated to no warning is filtered by district and period only (an occupancy record captured in the period). Example: "Kalutara Town Hall", registered by the Kalutara district officer during the event (UC3), is allocated to no warning, so its occupancy counts in this flood report whatever hazard brought its occupants. *Limitation (stated):* a shelter's capacity has no history, so the capacity line uses each matching shelter's capacity when the report is generated; a capacity changed after the period (UC3 A4) shows in a report generated afterwards, while a report already stored keeps its snapshot.
   - **Resource distribution** — the relief distributions made in the period to the selected districts in response to a Flood hazard, one distribution line per district, owner, supply category and unit. Quantities are never summed across units. A supply donated by a citizen is shown under "Private individual". Because a hazard type is selected, a distribution linked to no hazard is left out: 6,000 litres of water delivered to Kalutara on 22 May and recorded without a hazard link are not counted. The distribution lines are: Ratnapura · CARE Sri Lanka · Food · 1,200 dry-ration packs; Ratnapura · Sri Lanka Army · Water · 18,000 litres; Ratnapura · Private individual · Food · 480 dry-ration packs; Kalutara · Sri Lanka Red Cross Society · Shelter material · 350 tarpaulins; Kalutara · CARE Sri Lanka · Medicine · 220 first-aid kits.
8. System generates the report (*Generate Statistical Report*, which runs from here to step 12: the report is displayed at step 9 and kept open while the findings are recorded at steps 10–11, and step 12 is its extension point). It creates report "DR-2026-0184 · Flood response — Ratnapura, Kalutara, 20–25 May 2026" holding the generation time, the criteria and a snapshot of the results, with the number of matched records — the records retrieved across the selected metrics, each metric's calculator counting its own part and the parts added together: 1 warning (counted by the alert-timeline calculator only, although the citizens-reached calculator also resolves the matching warnings), 205,102 notifications (every notification of the matching warning retrieved, including the 32 sent to organisations and district officers: 102,549 of the Level 2 issue and 102,553 of the Level 3 escalation), 1,140 occupancy records and 5 distribution lines, 206,248 in all; unlike the citizens-reached figure, this count is of records, not of distinct citizens — and no failed metric. It links the hazard the report covers (Kalu Ganga flood, HZ-2026-0142). It checks the last synchronisation of every client in the selected districts and finds none still holding pending items. It stores the report as generated by the officer and records the audit record "report generated".
9. System displays the report scope, the number of matched records, the generation time and the results of each selected metric, with a chart for each: the timeline with the issue and the escalation, the per-channel and total reach, the occupancy curve against the combined-capacity line, and the distribution table and chart by district and by owner.
10. DMC Officer reviews the results and records the findings against the report: "The warning reached 42,335 citizens; SMS reached every registered citizen, the app channels 30,100. Shelter capacity was near its limit on 21 May (94 %), after the escalation to Level 3 – Red. Kalutara received no food distribution — review the stock pre-positioned with the Kalutara DDMCU."
11. System saves the findings with the report and shows the report with its findings.
12. DMC Officer requests an export of the report and selects the format PDF. This request is the extension point `export or share requested` of *Generate Statistical Report*; steps 12–19 are the behaviour of *Export or Share Report*, which extends it.
13. System produces the PDF file "DR-2026-0184_flood-response_2026-05.pdf" from the stored report with the exporter for that format, without recomputing any statistic (exports are tabular, without charts; a CSV file holds one section per metric). It records the audit record "report exported" and provides the file for download.
14. DMC Officer shares the report with the Resource-Owning Organisation CARE Sri Lanka.
15. System checks that the report holds distribution lines owned by CARE Sri Lanka (two lines). It records the organisation in the report's shared-with list, records the audit record "report shared", and shows the outcome: shared with CARE Sri Lanka; no organisation without data. No notification is sent to the organisation; it finds the report in its list of shared reports.
16. Later, in its own session, the Resource-Owning Organisation (CARE Sri Lanka) opens its list of shared reports in its report view.
17. System shows the reports shared with CARE Sri Lanka — the title, generation time, period and provisional status of each, without results — among them "DR-2026-0184 · Flood response — Ratnapura, Kalutara, 20–25 May 2026".
18. Resource-Owning Organisation opens report DR-2026-0184.
19. System checks that the report was shared with CARE Sri Lanka and shows the scoped report — its title, period (20–25 May 2026) and provisional status (final here; a provisional report shows its provisional districts and their last synchronisation times), with the results already restricted for CARE Sri Lanka by the server: the alert timeline, the citizens reached and the shelter occupancy are shown unchanged, and the distribution table contains only CARE Sri Lanka's two lines (Ratnapura · Food · 1,200 dry-ration packs; Kalutara · Medicine · 220 first-aid kits). The use case ends.

**Alternate Flows:**

**A1 — No records match the selected criteria** (at step 8)
- 8e. The computation at step 7 matched no records: the officer had chosen Kegalle, hazard type Landslide and 01–03 May 2026.
- 8f. System does not store the report and records no audit record; instead of the results it shows that no data matches the criteria, with a suggestion built from them: "No records for Kegalle, Landslide, 01–03 May 2026. Widen the districts or the period, or clear the hazard type."
- 8g. DMC Officer changes the criteria; continues at step 5.

**A2 — Report generated from a saved template** (at step 3)
- 3a. DMC Officer selects the saved criteria template "Western and Sabaragamuwa monsoon flood review" instead of an event. Templates are prepared in advance; this use case does not save new ones.
- 3b. System fills the criteria panel with the template's criteria: districts Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara, Ratnapura and Kegalle; hazard type Flood; the four metrics; the template's period 15 May – 30 September 2025.
- 3c. DMC Officer changes the period to 20–25 May 2026 and removes Colombo, Gampaha and Kegalle; continues at step 5.

**A3 — Some Districts Contain Unsynchronized Records** (at step 8)
- 8a. In this instance, unlike the main flow, the Kalutara district officer's browser still holds 3 items: System finds that this client in Kalutara reported 3 pending items at its last contact, on 25 May 2026 at 16:40.
- 8b. System generates the report from the records available on the server and marks it provisional for Kalutara since 25 May 2026 16:40 (for each provisional district, the earliest last synchronisation among its clients still holding pending items).
- 8c. At step 9, System shows the results with a provisional notice naming each affected district and its last successful synchronisation time: "Provisional — Kalutara: last synchronised 25 May 2026 16:40 with entries still pending on a device; Kalutara figures may be incomplete."
- 8d. DMC Officer continues reviewing the provisional report; continues at step 10. Once the client has synchronised, the officer can resubmit the same criteria (continues at step 6), which generates a final report as a new report.
- *Limitation (stated):* the rule relies on each client's pending count at its last contact. A device that went offline before capturing anything reported a pending count of 0 at that contact, so its district can show as final although data captured there later is still on the device.

**A4 — Criteria changed after reviewing the results** (at step 10)
- 10a. DMC Officer finds that the reporting scope needs adjustment (for example, Gampaha should be included).
- 10b. DMC Officer changes the criteria and resubmits them; continues at step 6. System generates a new report and keeps the earlier report unchanged.

**A5 — Report shared with several organisations** (at step 14)
- 14a. DMC Officer selects CARE Sri Lanka and Sri Lanka Red Cross Society.
- 14b. System checks each organisation's distribution lines in the report, records each organisation that owns lines in the report's shared-with list, records the audit record "report shared", and shows the outcome: shared with CARE Sri Lanka and Sri Lanka Red Cross Society.
- 14c. Each organisation finds the report in its list of shared reports and sees only its own distribution lines when it opens the report; continues at step 16.

**A6 — Criteria entered without a closed event** (at step 3)
- 3d. DMC Officer does not select an event and selects the districts, hazard type and period directly (the hazard type may be left empty to cover every hazard type); continues at step 5.

**A7 — Export or share not requested** (at steps 12 and 14; extension point `export or share requested`)
- 12a. DMC Officer does not request an export. If the officer shares the report, continues at step 14; otherwise the use case ends with the stored report and its findings.
- 14d. DMC Officer does not share the report; the use case ends.

**Postconditions:**
- The report is stored with its title, generation time, criteria, a snapshot of the results for every selected metric (including any metric that failed), the hazards it covers, its provisional districts with their last synchronisation times (none means final) and the officer who generated it. Every generation that matches records creates a new report; earlier reports are not replaced. A run that matches no records stores nothing (A1).
- The findings recorded by the officer are saved with the report.
- If an export was requested, the report has been exported in the requested format and provided for download, unless the export failed (E2).
- If sharing was requested, each organisation the report was shared with is in the report's shared-with list, the report appears in that organisation's list of shared reports, and its scoped view shows the report's title, period and provisional status and restricts the distribution table to the lines it owns.
- Audit records exist for every stored generation, every export (and every failed export) and every sharing.
- No warning, notification, shelter, occupancy record or relief distribution is changed by the reporting process.

**Exception Flows:**

*Technical failure (convention): if a server or network failure occurs on the analytics workspace or the organisation's report view, System shows that the service is unavailable and nothing was changed, and the actor may retry.*

**E1 — Invalid report criteria** (at step 6)
- 6a. The submitted criteria are invalid: start date 25 May 2026 after end date 20 May 2026, and no metric selected.
- 6b. System shows the validation errors next to the fields ("The start date is after the end date"; "Select at least one metric") and generates no report; the criteria panel keeps the values entered. The criteria are validated again when the officer confirms adjusted criteria (E4 6f); if they are no longer valid when confirmed (e.g. the officer's reporting districts have changed meanwhile), System shows the validation errors in the same way and generates no report; continues at step 6c.
- 6c. DMC Officer corrects the criteria; continues at step 5.

**E2 — Report export failure** (at step 13)
- 13a. The exporter for PDF fails while producing the file.
- 13b. System records the audit record "export failed" for the report and refuses the export (ExportFailedException).
- 13c. System shows the export error: "The PDF could not be produced. The report is saved — try again, or export as Excel or CSV."
- 13d. The report stays stored, so the export can be retried without regenerating the statistics; continues at step 12.

**E3 — One metric cannot be computed** (at step 7)
- 7a. The calculator for shelter occupancy over time fails (the occupancy records cannot be read).
- 7b. System records "shelter occupancy over time" in the report's failed metrics and computes the remaining metrics; the generation is not aborted.
- 7c. At step 8, System stores the report with the three computed metrics and the failed metric.
- 7d. At step 9, System shows the results of the three metrics and a computation error naming "shelter occupancy over time".
- 7e. DMC Officer continues reviewing; continues at step 10. To try the failed metric again later, the officer resubmits the criteria (continues at step 6), which generates a new report.

**E4 — Unauthorized District Requested** (at step 6)
- 6d. The criteria include Galle, which is not among the officer's reporting districts.
- 6e. System leaves Galle out and proposes adjusted criteria without it, showing the out-of-scope district and the reason: "Galle is outside your reporting districts (Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara, Ratnapura, Kegalle)."
- 6f. DMC Officer confirms the adjusted criteria; System validates them again (if they are no longer valid, as E1 6b) and generates the report with them; continues at step 7. Alternatively, the officer changes the criteria; continues at step 5.
- 6g. If none of the selected districts is in scope (for example, Galle alone), the criteria are invalid and no adjusted criteria are proposed; System shows the validation error "None of the selected districts is within your reporting districts" and continues as E1 (step 6c).

**E5 — An organisation's scoped report would contain no data** (at steps 14–15)
- 15a. DMC Officer also selects Sarvodaya, which owns no distribution line in the report.
- 15b. System does not share the report with Sarvodaya, and the outcome lists it under "no data — not shared" beside the organisations it was shared with.
- 15c. DMC Officer selects a different organisation (continues at step 14), changes the criteria (continues at step 5), or ends the use case.

**E6 — Organisation opens a report not shared with it** (at step 18)
- 18a. A Resource-Owning Organisation with which the report was not shared (Sarvodaya, after E5) does not find DR-2026-0184 in its list of shared reports and opens it by its report reference in its report view (opening by reference needs no list, because the scoped view carries the report's title and period itself).
- 18b. System refuses the scoped view (ReportNotSharedException) and shows that the report has not been shared with the organisation; no results are shown and the use case ends.
