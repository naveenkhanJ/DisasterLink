# Use Case: UC1 Issue Hazard Warning
**Use Case Scenario:** On 20 May 2026 at 10:45, DMC Officer N. Jayasinghe issues warning WRN-2026-0087, a Level 2 – Amber (Be Alert) flood warning for the Kalu Ganga flood hazard (HZ-2026-0142), targeted at Ratnapura District and the Kalu river basin (covered districts Ratnapura and Kalutara) and valid until 21 May 2026, 10:45; 42,335 registered citizens and 14 response organisations are alerted by push notification, audible alert and SMS in Sinhala, Tamil or English (102,549 notifications)

*Primary actor: DMC Officer (a DMC Duty Officer can also issue, because DMC Duty Officer specialises DMC Officer). Secondary actors: Push Notification Service, SMS Gateway (mobile operator) — through the included Broadcast Warning, whose receivers are the Citizen (including the Community Volunteer) and the Resource-Owning Organisation. Extension point: `warning needs modification` (extended by Modify Warning, alternate flow A4).*

**Description:**
This use case scenario describes how a DMC Officer at the DMC's National Emergency Operations Centre assesses a recorded hazard, sets the warning level on the NBRO three-level scale, defines the affected area by district and river basin, enters the message and emergency instructions in Sinhala, Tamil and English and previews and confirms the warning, and how the system broadcasts it to the registered citizens and response organisations of the covered districts by Push, SMS and Audible alert, recording the delivery status of every notification. It realises the included use cases Select Hazard Details (steps 4–5), Define Affected Area (step 7), Select Notification Channels (step 10) and Broadcast Warning (steps 14–16), and runs online only: the central dashboard has normal connectivity, and a warning is never saved offline.

*Conventions:* The Create Hazard Warning form and the pick-lists are parts of the dashboard screen and keep the values already entered whenever they are shown again; a server or network failure (server error, timeout, server unreachable) is shown by the screen's common error banner, changes nothing, and the officer may retry. An SMS carries the warning message followed by the emergency instructions in the recipient's language, separated by one line break that counts as one character, so the SMS length limit applies to the two together; the configured limit is three concatenated SMS parts per language — 201 characters for Sinhala and Tamil (Unicode, 67 per part) and 459 for English (GSM 7-bit, 153 per part) (Group_058 assumption). The English counter values are exact (116 + 1 + 122 = 239); the Sinhala and Tamil values are estimates, because the scenario does not reproduce those texts. A hazard is labelled on screen by its type and location name ("Flood — Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura"); "Kalu Ganga flood" is the scenario's narrative name. The SMS Gateway (mobile operator) reaches subscribers through the mobile operators' DEWN gateway.*

**Preconditions:**
- The DMC Officer is working at the DMC's National Emergency Operations Centre, where the central dashboard has normal network connectivity. (Login is outside the scope of this design.)
- At least one hazard is recorded with status ACTIVE. Every hazard has one of two sources: an agency bulletin (Department of Meteorology, Irrigation Department or NBRO), recorded in Record and Monitor Hazard, or a citizen report verified by a DMC Duty Officer in Submit and Verify Hazard Report. An unverified citizen report is never a hazard, so it cannot lead to a warning (CS-6).
- Citizens are registered with their district and preferred language (and a device token if they use the citizen app); response organisations are registered with the districts they serve and their preferred language.

**Main Flow:**
1. DMC Officer N. Jayasinghe opens the warning dashboard.
2. System retrieves the hazards that can be assessed — the hazards with status ACTIVE, each recorded from an agency bulletin or from a verified citizen report.
3. System displays the assessable hazards with their type, severity, districts, source and start time, for example "Flood — Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura, severity Medium – Ratnapura, Kalutara – agency bulletin – 20 May 2026, 09:30" (a hazard is labelled by its type and location name).
4. DMC Officer selects the Kalu Ganga flood hazard (HZ-2026-0142), shown as "Flood — Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura".
5. System displays the hazard details — type Flood, description "Kalu Ganga rising at the Ratnapura gauge; minor flood level exceeded", severity Medium, location name "Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura", districts Ratnapura and Kalutara, river basin Kalu, source agency bulletin, start time 20 May 2026, 09:30 — finds no missing information and no active warning for this hazard, and opens the Create Hazard Warning form for it.
6. DMC Officer assesses the hazard and chooses the warning level "Level 2 – Amber (Be Alert)" from the three NBRO levels offered.
7. DMC Officer defines the affected area by selecting the district Ratnapura and the river basin Kalu; the form's map highlights the selected area (the map only displays the selection).
8. DMC Officer sets the validity window: the warning expires on 21 May 2026 at 10:45 (24 hours).
9. DMC Officer enters the warning message and the emergency instructions in Sinhala, Tamil and English (English message: "DMC FLOOD WARNING - Level 2 Amber: Kalu Ganga rising in Ratnapura and Kalutara. Prepare to evacuate low-lying areas."; English instructions: "Move documents and valuables to higher ground. If told to evacuate, go to the safety centre named by your Grama Niladhari."); a character counter per language shows the message and instructions together against the configured SMS limit (Sinhala 188 / 201, Tamil 197 / 201, English 239 / 459).
10. DMC Officer selects the notification channels Push, SMS and Audible alert and chooses "Proceed to preview".
11. System validates the warning details (the hazard has no missing information; at least one district or river basin and at least one channel are selected; the message and instructions are present in all three languages; with SMS selected, each language's message and instructions together fit the configured SMS limit — 201 characters for Sinhala and Tamil in Unicode, 459 for English; the expiry time is after now) and displays the complete preview: hazard type Flood, level "Level 2 – Amber (Be Alert)", covered districts Ratnapura and Kalutara (Kalutara added by expanding the Kalu basin to its districts), the message and instructions in the three languages, the channels Push, SMS and Audible alert, the expiry time 21 May 2026, 10:45, and the recipient summary — 18,420 citizens in Ratnapura, 23,915 in Kalutara and 14 response organisations (district officers are notified only when a warning is escalated).
12. DMC Officer reviews the preview and confirms the broadcast ("Confirm & broadcast").
13. System validates the confirmed details again (E3 13g), checks again that the hazard has no active warning and records the warning under the hazard (WRN-2026-0087) with its level, message and instructions in three languages, target district Ratnapura, target river basin Kalu, channels, issue time 20 May 2026, 10:45, expiry time 21 May 2026, 10:45 and the issuing DMC Officer, N. Jayasinghe.
14. System identifies the recipients — the citizens and response organisations whose registered district is one of the covered districts, Ratnapura and Kalutara — creates one notification per recipient and per selected channel the recipient can receive, in the recipient's preferred language (citizens with the app: push, audible alert and SMS; citizens without the app and organisations: SMS; an SMS carries the message followed by the instructions), adds each notification to the warning and sends it through the gateway for its channel: push and audible alert through the Push Notification Service, SMS through the SMS Gateway.
15. Citizens and response organisations in Ratnapura and Kalutara receive the warning: the citizen app shows the push and audible-alert notifications of the warning as one alert in the citizen's language, with the alarm sound of the audible alert, even when the app is running in the background (design; the prototype shows the alert while the app is in the foreground — implementation standards §1); citizens without the app and the organisations receive the SMS.
16. System records the delivery status of each notification from the gateway's result — all 102,549 notifications DELIVERED (30,100 citizens with the app × 3 channels, 12,235 citizens by SMS only, 14 organisations by SMS).
17. System displays the warning as active on the dispatch status screen: warning WRN-2026-0087, Level 2 – Amber, covered districts Ratnapura and Kalutara, issued 10:45, expires 21 May 2026 10:45, 102,549 notifications delivered, no failures.
18. DMC Officer monitors the active warning: later that day (16:30) the officer selects the Kalu Ganga flood hazard (HZ-2026-0142) on the dashboard again.
19. System displays the hazard details with the active warning WRN-2026-0087 and its current dispatch status, retrieved again from the recorded notifications (102,549 delivered, no failures), and offers Escalate for that warning (Escalate Warning, alternate flow A1, started only by the officer's own choice). The warning stays active until its expiry time, 21 May 2026, 10:45, after which the system shows it as expired without any action by the officer; the use case ends.

**Alternate Flows:**

**A1 — Hazard Information Is Insufficient** (at step 5)
- 5a. System finds that the selected hazard lacks information required for a warning — the Kalu Ganga hazard was recorded from the bulletin without its affected districts — and displays the notice "A warning cannot be issued until the missing information is provided: affected districts", without opening the warning form.
- 5b. DMC Officer updates the hazard from the hazard details panel: enters the districts Ratnapura and Kalutara, keeping the description and severity Medium.
- 5c. System validates and records the hazard update, shows the updated hazard and opens the Create Hazard Warning form; continues at step 6. (An invalid update: E5.)

**A2 — Affected area not selected** (at step 11)
- 11a. System's validation finds that the officer chose "Proceed to preview" without selecting any district or river basin.
- 11b. System displays the notice "An affected area is required: select at least one district or river basin" on the form; no preview is shown, and the form keeps the details already entered.
- 11c. DMC Officer selects the district Ratnapura and the river basin Kalu and chooses "Proceed to preview" again; continues at step 11 (the details are validated again).

**A4 — Warning Is Modified Before Broadcasting** (at step 12; extension point `warning needs modification`, extended by Modify Warning)
- 12a. DMC Officer finds incorrect or incomplete information in the preview — the Tamil instructions do not mention the safety centre — and chooses "Back & edit".
- 12b. System reopens the Create Hazard Warning form, which keeps the details already entered.
- 12c. DMC Officer corrects the Tamil instructions and chooses "Proceed to preview".
- 12d. System validates the modified details again and displays the regenerated preview; continues at step 12 (the officer confirms the updated warning).

**A5 — Hazard already has an active warning** (at step 5)
- 5d. System's hazard details show that the hazard already has an active warning — WRN-2026-0081, Level 1 – Yellow (Remain Vigilant), issued at 09:50 — with its current dispatch status, and offer Escalate instead of opening the warning form (a hazard has at most one active warning).
- 5e. DMC Officer chooses Escalate, which opens the escalation screen for WRN-2026-0081 (Escalate Warning, as at its A1 1e); this use case ends.

**Postconditions:**
- The warning is recorded under its hazard with its level (NBRO scale), message and emergency instructions in Sinhala, Tamil and English, target districts and river basins, channels, issue time, expiry time and issuing DMC Officer; it is the hazard's only active warning.
- One notification exists for each recipient and each selected channel the recipient can receive, in the recipient's preferred language, each with status DELIVERED or FAILED, plus any resend notifications in the same round (E1); the officer has been shown any failed recipients.
- The registered citizens and response organisations of the covered districts (river basins expanded to their districts) are informed of the hazard and the recommended emergency actions.
- The warning is displayed as active on the dashboard and becomes expired automatically at its expiry time.
- If the use case ends without issuing a warning (A5, E2 when the first attempt was not recorded, E6 after another officer's warning), no warning is recorded and nothing is broadcast. After E2 with a lost reply followed by E6, the warning recorded by the first attempt stands as in the postconditions above.

**Exception Flows:**

**E1 — Notification Service Failure** (at step 16)
- 16a. The SMS Gateway (mobile operator) reports failure for 1,240 SMS notifications to numbers in Kalutara (a mobile operator outage); the push and audible-alert notifications are delivered. (Scenario data, not shown on the screen: of the 1,240, 830 are citizens with the app and 410 citizens who receive SMS only.)
- 16b. System records those 1,240 notifications as FAILED and the other 101,309 as DELIVERED; the warning stays recorded and active.
- 16c. System displays the active warning with its dispatch status and alerts the officer: "SMS delivery failed for 1,240 recipients", showing per channel the recipients whose notification failed (SMS: the 1,240; Push and Audible alert: none) and any unreachable recipients (none); the screen makes no claim about who was reached on another channel.
- 16d. When the SMS Gateway is available again (the operator reports the outage over), DMC Officer chooses SMS as the channel for the failed recipients and resends. (The officer picks the alternative channel of Group_060's E1.4: SMS again once the SMS Gateway is available, or Push, which reaches only the citizens with the app.)
- 16e. System resends the warning on SMS (message followed by instructions) to the recipients with a recipient–channel pair whose latest notification in the current round is FAILED, and who have no DELIVERED notification on SMS in that round — the 1,240 recipients; all 1,240 are delivered. System records each delivery status and displays the updated dispatch status: 101,309 + 1,240 = 102,549 notifications delivered, no failures, nobody unreachable; continues at step 18.

**E2 — Central System Becomes Temporarily Unavailable** (at step 13)
- 13a. The confirmation does not reach the central system, the system fails before the warning is recorded, or the reply is lost.
- 13b. System shows that the service is unavailable (the screen's common error banner): as far as the officer can tell, the warning was not issued and nothing was broadcast; the confirmed details stay on the preview. The warning is not queued offline (Issue Hazard Warning runs online only).
- 13c. DMC Officer confirms again when the system is available; continues at step 13 (if the first attempt had in fact recorded the warning, E6 applies).

**E3 — Invalid warning information** (at step 11; at step 13 when the confirmed details are validated again)
- 11d. System's validation finds invalid or missing details: the Tamil message and instructions together have 236 characters, more than the configured 201 (three Unicode SMS parts), and the Sinhala instructions are empty.
- 11e. System displays the validation messages beside the fields ("Tamil message and instructions: 236 / 201 characters", "Sinhala instructions are required"); no preview is shown, and the form keeps the details already entered.
- 11f. DMC Officer shortens the Tamil instructions, enters the Sinhala instructions and chooses "Proceed to preview" again; continues at step 11 (the details are validated again).
- 13g. If the details are no longer valid when confirmed (e.g. the expiry time has passed while the preview was open), System shows the validation errors on the form, which keeps the details already entered, and the preview is not issued — no warning is recorded and nothing is broadcast; the officer corrects the details as in 11f; continues at step 11.

**E4 — Hazard situation changes before broadcasting** (at step 12; the warning is changed in 12f–12h through extension point `warning needs modification`, extended by Modify Warning, as in A4)
- 12e. Before the officer confirms, an Irrigation Department bulletin (10:40) reports the Kalu Ganga at Ratnapura above major flood level, so the previewed warning is no longer accurate.
- 12f. DMC Officer chooses "Back & edit" and updates the hazard: severity High, description "Kalu Ganga above major flood level at the Ratnapura gauge", districts Ratnapura and Kalutara; System validates and records the hazard update and shows the updated hazard on the form. (An invalid update: E5.)
- 12g. DMC Officer changes the level to "Level 3 – Red (Evacuate)" and the message and instructions in the three languages accordingly, and chooses "Proceed to preview".
- 12h. System validates the details again and displays the regenerated preview; continues at step 12 (the officer confirms the updated warning).

**E5 — Hazard update is invalid** (at step 5c of A1 or 12f of E4)
- 5f / 12i. System refuses the hazard update — for example, no district is given — and displays the validation messages ("At least one district is required"); the hazard is not changed.
- 5g / 12j. DMC Officer corrects the update and submits it again. On the A1 path the flow continues at step 5c (the System validates and records the update). On the E4 path the System validates and records the corrected update and shows the updated hazard on the form, then continues at step 12g.

**E6 — Hazard received an active warning in the meantime** (at step 13)
- 13d. System finds that the hazard already has an active warning — issued by another officer after step 5, or recorded by the first attempt in E2.
- 13e. System refuses to record a second warning, displays the existing active warning (number, level, issue time) and states that it can be escalated instead; nothing is broadcast.
- 13f. DMC Officer chooses Escalate, which opens the escalation screen for that warning (Escalate Warning, as at its A1 1e), or leaves the dashboard; this use case ends.

## Dictionary gaps

Gaps resolved in P60: former gap 1 (no display for a failed server call) by C1; gap 2 (`showWarningForm` keeps the entered details) by C2; gap 3 (`WarningPreview.expiryTime`) by N1; gap 4 (recipients skipped by a resend, `BroadcastSummary.unreachableRecipientIds`) by N2; gap 5 (SMS content and the length rule) by N3; gap 6 (the hazard details offer Escalate for an active warning) by N8; gap 7 (`WarningController.getBroadcastSummary`) by N4.

1. **`HazardUpdate` cannot supply every item `missingInformation()` may report.** It holds only `description`, `severity` and `districts`; a hazard missing its river basin, location or location name cannot be completed in A1. A1's example therefore uses missing districts. **Resolved (MD v0.7.1/v0.8):** `missingInformation()` reports only items that `HazardUpdate` can supply.
2. **The configured SMS limit has no stated value.** N3 applies the limit to message + instructions; the dictionary still gives the single-SMS sizes (70 Unicode / 160 GSM), with which no realistic message and instructions fit. The scenario assumes a configured limit of three concatenated parts (201 Unicode / 459 GSM characters per language); MD (`validateWarning`, `validateEscalation`) or the implementation standards should state the configured value. **Resolved (MD v0.7.1/v0.8):** MD states the configured limit, 201 / 459 characters.
